# Features and background work

Details moved out of the README. Each paragraph describes one area of the app.

**Job finder** (sidebar; the listings can be filtered by status and by source) pulls PHP/Laravel listings into a review list. Each source has its own worker: the Himalayas search API, We Work Remotely's programming RSS feeds, and, by scraping their pages, LaraJobs, Wellfound and Built In (these can block or break without notice and may be against those sites' terms; use at your own discretion). LaraJobs' home page lists every open posting in one request, and since it is a Laravel-only board there (like Built In, which searches server-side) the keyword filter does not apply; each card also carries the employer's own application link, which is kept in the listing notes. Remote country or timezone suffixes are checked against the eligible regions; hybrid and office locations with unknown eligibility remain in the review list for manual assessment. Runs start only from the buttons on the page and never on a schedule. A run makes at most `SCRAPER_MAX_PAGES` requests (default 3) per source, `SCRAPER_SPACING` seconds apart (default 5), only one run can be queued or running at a time, and any refusal (403, 429, timeouts) ends that run immediately. Listings that mention none of the `config/scrapers.php` keywords, junior/intern titles, listings restricted to places outside the US (`allowed_regions`; ones with no restriction are labelled Worldwide), and links already staged or tracked are skipped. Found listings wait on the page: tick any number of rows (the ticks survive paging) and press Add selected listings to save them through the normal save path, with the source named and every field the site gave (company page, location, eligible countries and time zones, pay, requirements, notes, posting date and expiry); the selection is cleared afterwards and unticked listings stay. Pay in another currency is converted to US dollars when a listing is staged, at the fixed approximate rates in `config/scrapers.php` (`usd_rates`; a currency not listed is left as posted), with the original figures kept in the pay notes. The sort and filter choices stay in the page address. Both the review list and the overview can be sorted by pay (hourly, monthly and other periods are scaled to a year, currencies are not converted, unknown pay last) and by location (US first, then Worldwide, then A–Z); the review list also sorts by posting date. Dismissed ones are remembered and not found again.

**Queue** (sidebar) is one page for background work, with an All / Link checks / Grading filter (kept in the URL; `/link-checks` and `/scoring` redirect to it). The two sections below describe what each does. **Link checks** fetch an application page in the background to see whether it is still live (404/410 are flagged as gone, and so are pages that load but say the job is gone, such as "job not found", or whose structured data has expired; sites that refuse automated requests, such as LinkedIn, are flagged as blocked) and fill in only the fields that are still empty from the page's structured job data (schema.org JobPosting). Links on Ashby, Greenhouse and Lever are checked through those companies' public job-board APIs instead of their pages (the pages are JavaScript shells that answer 200 even for a closed job), which also fills title, location, pay and the description. Nothing existing is overwritten. A job whose link is gone (404/410) is closed automatically, unless it is already applied or past the saved/preparing stages; other failures are only flagged. Check one job from its page, all active jobs from the log page, or paste job page links on the Import page to create new listings from them. The log page can clear finished entries (older than 30 or 7 days, or all); queued and running checks and ones whose AI request is running are kept, a waiting AI request is cancelled with its check, and jobs are never touched. Checks are spaced a few seconds apart (`LINKCHECK_SPACING`), never run on a schedule, and refuse private or reserved network addresses.

**Mail leads** (`/mail-leads` in the sidebar; old `/linkedin` bookmarks redirect here) collects job links from email. Its **Scan mailbox** button starts an on-demand mailbox scan; `php artisan mail:scan` on the host does the same. It reads an IMAP mailbox read-only (`BODY.PEEK`: nothing is marked read, moved or deleted), looks at the last `MAIL_SCAN_DAYS` days of `MAIL_SCAN_FOLDER`, keeps links to known job sites (the `job_links` allowlist in `config/mailscan.php`, stripped of tracking parameters) and queues them as link checks, which create new listings. LinkedIn job-alert digests are read directly instead, since LinkedIn refuses automated fetches: each card is kept as a link (title, company, location, notes such as "actively hiring", alert name and the canonical `linkedin.com/jobs/view/<id>` address) in the **Mail leads** list, and nothing is requested from LinkedIn. A card never becomes a half-filled listing by itself. The **Mail leads** page lists the kept links; tick the ones you want and press *Scan selected*, and a host-side reader (`bin/linkedin-scan`, `tools/linkedin/scan.mjs`) opens each in a Chrome profile you signed into once (`bin/linkedin-scan --login`), reads title, company, location, description and any external apply link, and creates the listing through the normal save path, marking the link imported with a pointer to it. When the signed-in scan finds an external Apply link it becomes the listing's application link (the LinkedIn posting moves to an extra field); an Easy Apply job keeps the LinkedIn link and is noted as Easy Apply, and imported links without an external link show a *Rescan* button. The AI extraction also asks for the employer's own apply link: it is used only when that exact address appears in the page text as a whole address, is not a linkedin.com address, would not replace a link you set, and is not already another listing's link; it must be one whole address written in the text, then replaces the LinkedIn application link and the posting moves to the extra fields. The listing is marked "Apply link source" until you press *Confirm apply link* on its page, and the agent apply run (Draft resume and letter) stays off until then. The scanned description is also saved as a link check and queued for the AI extraction, which splits it into requirements, benefits and the other fields (the earlier text is kept on the AI request). Links are spaced 20 to 60 seconds apart (`LINKEDIN_SCAN_DELAY_MIN`/`MAX`) so a batch is not a burst, the browser is headed on purpose because LinkedIn blocks headless sessions, and a page that asks for a sign-in marks the link failed with the reason instead of retrying forever. `php artisan linkedin:links --json` still prints the waiting links for anything else. A link whose job is already tracked is not kept again, and Dismiss removes one. Links to jobs already tracked are skipped. `php artisan mail:scan --dry-run` lists what would be created or queued and records nothing. Each email's Message-ID, sender, subject and link counts are remembered, so it is never read twice; bodies are not stored. Set `MAIL_SCAN_USERNAME` and `MAIL_SCAN_PASSWORD` (an iCloud app-specific password; `MAIL_SCAN_HOST` defaults to `imap.mail.me.com`) in `.env`. When the mailbox is configured, the `scheduler` compose service (`schedule:work`) runs `mail:scan` every 30 minutes without overlap; the first real connection is an outbound action for the owner to approve. The LinkedIn reader runs as the host user unit `docker/jacker-linkedin-scan.service` (`bin/linkedin-scan --loop`, link it into `~/.config/systemd/user/` and enable it). On a sign-in wall (scan exit 5) the worker marks that row failed and stops without restarting; Mail leads shows a sign-in banner, and after `bin/linkedin-scan --login` and starting the unit, *Retry failed* requeues the failed rows.

**Saved pages and pasted text.** The Import page also has *Add from a saved page or text* (one file box: `.csv`/`.tsv` start the CSV import, `.html`/`.htm`/`.mhtml`/`.mht`/`.txt` are imported as a posting): save a job page in your browser (HTML or MHTML), or paste the posting text, and import it with no network request. Structured job data is read at once; otherwise the text is kept and queued for AI reading. Choose an existing job to fill its empty fields, or leave it empty to create a new listing. PDF files are not read yet (no PDF text tool is installed).

**Render page.** Pages that build their content with JavaScript give the plain fetch almost no text. *Read with AI* on a fetched row first queues a browser render of its link, and the AI pass waits for it (and still runs on the old text if the render fails): the host bridge (`bin/ai-bridge`) opens the link in headless Chromium through Playwright, waits for the page to settle and replaces the check's saved text with what the browser shows; The AI then reads that text. It is on demand only (never in bulk or on a schedule), skips saved text, and does not load images, media, fonts or stylesheets. All browser traffic, redirects, scripts and WebSockets included, goes through a local filtering proxy (`tools/render/proxy.mjs`) that connects only to the public addresses the app's address guard returns (`php artisan render:allow`), so a name cannot be re-resolved to a private address after it was checked, and localhost and LAN targets are refused. WebRTC is switched off (browser flags plus removing the API from every page) so no UDP can bypass the proxy. Chromium also runs in its own network namespace with only a loopback interface (`tools/render/isolated-chrome.sh`, via unprivileged `unshare`); the proxy listens on a unix socket and a small relay (`relay.mjs`) forwards the namespace's loopback port to it, so the browser has no route anywhere else. `RENDER_ISOLATE=0` falls back to a plain loopback proxy where unprivileged namespaces are unavailable. Chromium runs with its sandbox on (`RENDER_NO_SANDBOX=1` turns it off where unprivileged user namespaces are unavailable). One-time host setup: `cd tools/render && npm install`; it uses the newest Chromium in `~/.cache/ms-playwright` (or `RENDER_CHROME`) and `RENDER_TIMEOUT_MS` (default 30000). `node --test` in `tools/render` runs the proxy tests. The systemd unit below runs it.

**Read with AI (host bridge by default).** When a live page has no structured data, the link-check log keeps its readable text (the text is kept for every fetched page, with or without structured data, so you can also request an AI pass on a page whose structured data was incomplete; a pass fills empty fields and may replace the long text fields (benefits, responsibilities, requirements, hours), keeping the earlier values under *Replaced earlier values* in the log. Pages read through a job-board API keep the posting text the API returned) (capped by `LINKCHECK_MAX_TEXT_CHARS`) and offers a *Read with AI* button. Clicking it queues one request (`ai_requests`) and the row shows its state (queued, reading, then the result or the failure reason; the page refreshes itself, and a failed request can be retried, and the model's raw reply is kept and shown under *Raw reply*); nothing runs in bulk or on a schedule. A host-side bridge (`bin/ai-bridge`, needs `docker`, `jq` and `sessioneer` with `--wait` and `--no-tools` on the host; run it by hand, or with `--loop`; `docker/jacker-ai-bridge.service` is a systemd user unit that runs the loop: symlink it into `~/.config/systemd/user/`, edit the `ExecStart` path to your checkout, then `systemctl --user daemon-reload && systemctl --user enable --now jacker-ai-bridge`) takes queued requests through `php artisan ai:claim`, runs each one through the agent chain in `config/ai.php` (Claude Haiku via a one-off Sessioneer session started with `--no-tools`, then Codex in a read-only sandbox, then opencode with its tools denied; an agent with no quota or an error falls through to the next, `AI_MODEL`, `AI_CODEX_MODEL` and `AI_OPENCODE_MODEL` set the models, and the bridge skips any entry that is not one of those three agents with a plain model name) and returns the first usable reply, and which agent gave it, through `php artisan ai:complete <id>`, so a posting that tries to give the model instructions cannot make it run anything; the prompt also says the page is data only and the page cannot close the text fence early. Only valid job fields are accepted, and only empty fields of the job are filled, apart from the long text fields an AI pass may replace.

**Settings** (sidebar) hold these things. *Profile links* are an optional LinkedIn and GitHub address (https, on linkedin.com / github.com) shown in the top bar when set; a blank field hides it. *PDF header* (name, email, phone, location; blank lines are left out) and *Cover letter AI* (agent claude, codex or opencode, a plain model name and a thinking level off, low, medium or high) belong to cover letters, below. *Grading criteria* edit the criteria below without touching code: the skill-match rubric, the pay weight, points for unknown pay, the Eastern-hours penalty, the pay bands and the points per work arrangement. They are stored in the `settings` table (`ScoringSettings`, falling back to `config/scoring.php`); *Reset to defaults* deletes them. Changes apply to the next grading run.

**Cover letters.** Each job has one letter on its detail page: paste the text or import a .txt / .md file, edit it and save (a blank save removes it). Saving is local unless the optional similarity feature is enabled. *Download PDF* (`/opportunities/{id}/cover-letter.pdf`, 404 without a letter) renders it through `App\Services\MarkdownPdf` (Liberation Sans, the same renderer and header as the materials section; text containing CV or References answers 422 with the reason). *Suggest improvements with AI* queues an `ai_requests` row of kind `letter`; the host bridge takes it with `php artisan letter:claim`, runs the single agent, model and thinking level saved in Settings (re-checked against the same allowlists, claude through `sessioneer --no-tools`, codex read-only, opencode with tools denied) and stores the plain-text reply with `letter:complete`. The prompt fences the job listing and the letter between marker lines with a fresh random token as data only. The suggestions are kept on the letter with a timestamp (a new run replaces them) and are only displayed: the AI never edits the letter. This is the default bridge path; the optional Provider API path runs in the queue worker.

**Grading** re-calculates *fit* (skill match) and *want* (desirability) for open listings (not closed, archived or applied). *Score unscored* only takes listings missing a score; *Re-score all open* overwrites every score after a confirmation, and the page keeps each listing's earlier scores in its Before column. Fit is judged from the listing by an AI agent using the rubric in `config/scoring.php`; want is computed in PHP from pay (annualised midpoint, neutral when unknown) and work mode, minus a penalty for Eastern-only hours; by default 60% pay and 40% work mode and one point off, all editable under Grading settings. Requests are `ai_requests` rows of kind `score`, taken by the host bridge by default (`php artisan score:claim` / `score:complete`), which tries `config('scoring.chain')` in order (Claude Haiku through `sessioneer --no-tools`, then `codex exec --sandbox read-only` (user config and rules ignored), then `opencode run` with shell, edit, web, subagent, skill and outside-directory access denied and an empty config directory so no MCP servers load; `SCORE_MODEL`, `SCORE_CODEX_MODEL` and `SCORE_OPENCODE_MODEL` change the models) until one returns a JSON reply, so an agent out of quota falls through to the next. *Clear done* removes finished requests; the scores stay. Nothing runs on a schedule.

**Questions** (sidebar) is one list of interview questions to ask, filtered by stage (applied, screening, interviewing, offer; the stage is the opportunity status). Add, edit, reorder or delete them there, optionally with predefined answers (one per line). Each opportunity has a *Questions* page (button on its detail page) where you record the answers as you go; it opens on the job's current status, and clearing a field means the question does not apply. A job in *Recruiter contact* (a recruiter reached out before you applied; not counted as applied, and the company field can hold the recruiter's name until you know the employer) opens on the screening questions. The initial list is loaded once with `php artisan db:seed --class=InterviewQuestionSeeder`.

## AI driver selection and similarities

Settings selects a processing method per feature: page extraction, job grading, and letter suggestions. The host bridge remains the default. The optional Provider API path uses Laravel AI with Anthropic or OpenAI credentials configured on the server. Saving settings does not call a provider; future explicit AI requests use the selected method. The same response validation and letter-draft protection apply to both paths. A live page still needs the host bridge for browser rendering before extraction. See [configuration](configuration.md#ai-processing) and [architecture](architecture.md#ai-requests).

The optional similarity experiment indexes job text and saved draft letters with embeddings. After enabling it, click **Index existing jobs**; later content edits queue updates. The detail page then links to similar active jobs and relevant past letters. Comparison runs locally, excludes stale or incompatible index entries, and never adds a previous letter to an AI prompt automatically. Embedding generation sends source text to the configured provider and may incur charges. It is off by default.

**Private search** (Job finder, optional): a *Run private search* button queues an `ai_requests` row of kind `scrape`. The host agent worker (`bin/agent-worker`, separate from the tool-less `bin/ai-bridge`) claims it with `agent:claim`, launches a tools-enabled Sessioneer Claude session inside a local ai-job-search checkout to run its `/scrape` and `/rank`, has it write a CSV with Jacker's own headers (`company,title,application_url,location,deadline,notes,skill_match`), imports it with `csv:import` (exact URL duplicates are skipped) and records the result with `agent:complete`. The button appears only while the worker is running and has found the repo, `docker`, `jq`, `python3` and `sessioneer` (it heartbeats with `agent:register`; no heartbeat for 90 seconds hides it), so a clone without them never shows it. One run at a time; a run reaches live job sites and can take many minutes, so it starts only from the button. Nothing is applied or sent. Every task is wrapped in markers carrying a fresh random token, and the session is told that only text between them is an instruction and that anything it fetches is data, the same convention the AI readers use for listings.

**Application materials** (job detail page) merge the cover letter box and the draft panel into one section with Resume and Cover letter tabs (`?materials=letter` opens the letter). Each tab has a version picker (resume: edited, generated, general; letter: edited, generated, none; only versions that exist are offered, preferring edited, then generated, then general), a Markdown box with a short syntax help, Save, Preview (inline) and Download PDF. Edited text is rendered on request by `App\Services\MarkdownPdf` (Liberation Sans, text layer) through `/opportunities/{id}/materials/{resume|letter}/{generated|edited|general}` (`?download=1` for an attachment); generated and general PDFs are served as stored (general only for the resume). Text containing the word CV or References, empty text or more than 20,000 characters cannot be rendered: saving still succeeds and the page shows the renderer's message, and the PDF route answers 422 with it. A blank save deletes the edited version (`SaveResume`, `SaveCoverLetter`). The Draft resume and letter button and its status live in the same section. **Mark as used** (same section): saves a frozen copy of the PDFs for the versions currently picked in the Resume and Cover letter tabs under `storage/app/agent/apply/{id}/used/{UTC timestamp}/` (`resume.pdf`, `letter.pdf`; edited versions are rendered at that moment, generated and general ones are copied byte for byte) and records `resume_choice`, `letter_choice` and `materials_used_at` on the job in one transaction (`App\Actions\MarkMaterialsUsed`). Later edits or regeneration never change the frozen copy; marking again keeps the older folders and records the latest. A missing source, a renderer rejection or nothing picked saves nothing and shows the reason; the job's status is not changed. The detail page shows what was used and opens the frozen PDFs (`/opportunities/{id}/materials/used/{resume|letter}`). The three fields are exported read-only in the CSV and ignored on import (`docs/csv.md`). Copy: `lang/en/materials.php`. Tests: tests/Feature/ApplicationMaterialsTest.php, tests/Feature/MarkMaterialsUsedTest.php.

**Custom resume and cover letter** (job detail page, optional, same worker): *Draft resume and letter* queues an `apply` request for that one job (it needs an http(s) application link; one pending per job, never in bulk). The worker runs the private `/apply` workflow on that link, checks that the reply names a PDF under `cv/` and one under `cover_letters/`, and copies them to `storage/app/agent/apply/{job id}/`; it also asks for plain Markdown versions (`cv/*.md`, `cover_letters/*.md`, validated like the PDFs, symlinks refused) stored as `resume.md` and `letter.md` there, and `agent:preload` fills the job's empty letter box and a new per-job `resumes` row (Markdown body) from them, only where nothing exists yet (existing letter or resume text is never overwritten; missing, blank, non-UTF-8, over 20,000 characters or symlinked files are skipped and the PDFs still stand); the detail page's Application materials section serves the PDFs (only through the app, never from `public/`). They contain your contact details and are drafts for you to review: you apply yourself, and nothing is sent.

**Markdown PDF renderer** (used by the Application materials section on the job detail page): turns resume or letter Markdown into a Liberation Sans PDF with a real text layer; see `docs/architecture.md`, "Markdown to PDF".
